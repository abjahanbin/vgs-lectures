Chapter 1

I’m Your Developer

You are the designers and writers.

I build what you specify.

Your job: design something buildable, and describe it clearly.

A character-oriented experience.

Mechanical AgencyNarrative Agency
Physical level design: where you go, what you open, what you carryBranching dialogue: who you talk to, what you say, what changes
Your Project 2 skillsYour Project 1 skills
The system responds to what you doThe story responds to what you choose
Puzzle Games Card Games RPGs

Great genres. Too big for this build.

Lock-and-key puzzles are fine. A whole puzzle game is not.

Design inside the menu. Describe it so I can build it.

Chapter 4

Character First

IMAGE: a frame where the hero is out of focus / cropped and a background character is sharp

You are never the hero. You’re at the edge of the frame.

Who are you?

The minion, the shopkeeper, the hero’s horse

What do you want that the hero’s story ignores?

What can’t you do?

Verbs Are Character

CharacterCanCan’t

Messengerrun, deliver, talkopen sealed doors

What you can’t do is part of the argument.

Pick the character before the mechanics.

Reset

The hero cleared the dungeon last night. You’re the caretaker, and you have until dawn to put it all back.

IMAGE: key art — mop + key ring in lantern light
1

Title & Logline

“Reset.”
Whose story? The hero’s. You mop up after it.

2

Theme & Rhetoric

Progress, subverted. Every bit of the hero’s progress undoes your work. They meet at one mechanic: relocking the gate you watched the hero open.

Ludus (goals, timer); agon vs. the clock, with some mimicry. The magic circle is the night shift.
